Part Three

Review findings

Each item below compares a statement in the documents against the code, the permissions the app requests, or the services it actually sends data to. Ordered by how likely it is to matter at review.

The single most important one: the Play Data safety form and the Apple App Privacy label are both cross-checked against this policy. Findings 1, 2 and 5 are all cases where the policy says less than the app does — which is the specific shape of mismatch that gets a submission sent back.
01 Should fix Privacy · §4 Third-Party Services
Several services the app actually uses are not disclosed
The policy names Firebase, MongoDB Atlas and a generic "AI Services". The app also sends data to: Sentry (crash reports, including IP address), Cloudinary (every uploaded trade screenshot), Firebase Cloud Messaging (push tokens), Resend (OTP and transactional email), and Google Gemini (the unnamed "AI Services" — your trade notes are sent to it). Razorpay applies once payments are switched on.
Why it matters: Play's Data safety form asks you to declare third-party sharing, and Apple cross-checks the App Privacy label against your policy. An omission here is the most common cause of a policy being rejected as inaccurate.
02 Should fix Privacy · §1 What We Do NOT Collect
"We do not collect device identifiers" contradicts the app
The app collects an x-device-id per install and stores FCM push notification tokens in a DeviceToken collection. Both are device identifiers. The qualifier "for tracking purposes" is doing a lot of work here.
Why it matters: You will have to declare Device ID on the Play Data safety form. A form that says "collected" against a policy that says "not collected" is a direct contradiction reviewers do check.
03 Should fix Terms · §6 Refund Policy
"All payments are final and non-refundable" cannot hold once you use store billing
With Apple In-App Purchase, refunds are handled by Apple and users request them from Apple directly — your terms cannot override that. Google Play has its own refund policy plus statutory rights. India's Consumer Protection Act also applies.
Why it matters: The "except where required by applicable law" carve-out softens it, but once billing runs through a store the clause is misleading. Reword to defer to the store's refund process for store-billed purchases.
04 Should fix Terms · missing
No Apple-required EULA clauses
If you ship your own terms instead of Apple's standard EULA, Apple requires them to state that the agreement is between you and the user (not Apple), that Apple has no support or maintenance obligation, and that Apple is a third-party beneficiary entitled to enforce the terms.
Why it matters: Required by the Apple Developer Program License Agreement (Schedule A) whenever a custom EULA is used. Straightforward to add, and a known rejection trigger.
05 Worth fixing Privacy · missing
Push notifications and camera access are never mentioned
The app requests notification permission and camera access (Android CAMERA, and the iOS NSCameraUsageDescription string added for submission). Neither appears anywhere in the policy.
Why it matters: Apple compares your usage-description strings and the App Privacy label against the policy. Permissions the app requests but the policy never explains read as undisclosed collection.
06 Worth fixing Privacy · missing
IP addresses and server logs not disclosed
The backend records IP address with each session and login (visible in the auth diagnostics and request logs), and Sentry receives it too. The policy does not mention log data at all.
Why it matters: IP is personal data under both GDPR and India's DPDP Act. Cheap to disclose, awkward to be caught omitting.
07 Worth fixing Terms · §6 Subscription & Payments
Describes billing the app currently cannot perform
Monthly / 3-Month / 6-Month plans are described as purchasable, but the paywall is switched off in the shipped build (NEXT_PUBLIC_PAYMENTS_ENABLED=false) and there is no /pricing route.
Why it matters: Not fatal — terms may describe future plans. But a reviewer reading "requires a paid subscription" and finding no way to pay may question it. Consider marking the section as applying "where subscriptions are offered".
08 Worth fixing Terms · §9 Account Termination
"Cancel your subscription before deleting your account" has no cancel flow
The app has no subscription management or cancellation UI, so this instruction cannot be followed from inside the app.
Why it matters: Play expects account deletion not to strand an active subscription. Once billing is live, either add cancellation or cancel automatically on deletion — and say which.
09 Worth fixing Terms · §12 Governing Law
Governing law names no jurisdiction
"Governed by applicable laws" and "submitted to the appropriate jurisdiction" identify nothing. As drafted the clause has close to no legal effect.
Why it matters: Name the country and the courts (e.g. "the laws of India, courts at "). Everything else in the app — INR pricing, IST scheduling, Indian Market module — points to India already.
10 Worth fixing Privacy · missing
No Indian data-protection framing and no grievance contact
India's DPDP Act 2023 and the IT Rules expect a named grievance contact for user complaints. The policy gives a general support address only.
Why it matters: Given an India-based operation and an Indian user base, naming a grievance officer and referencing the DPDP Act is the expected form.
11 Check Both · Contact
A personal phone number is published
+91 90616 50463 appears at the bottom of both documents.
Why it matters: Intentional is fine — just confirm you want it public on a page the stores link to, since it will be scraped.
12 Check Both · Header
Dates now current, naming now consistent
Both documents read "Last Updated: August 6, 2026" and refer to "Edgecipline" throughout. The earlier "Edge Discipline" references are gone.
Why it matters: Noted as resolved — no action needed.
